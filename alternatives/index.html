Free screen recorders compared: honest pros and cons

Updated 22 October 2026 · 11-minute read

Most free screen recorders have a catch: a watermark, a five-minute limit, a cap on how many videos you can make, or an upload to someone else's servers. This page puts eleven of them side by side, including the recorders built into Chromebooks, Macs and Windows, so you can spot the catch before you start. We make Dotfik Studio, so every fact about the others comes from their own pages, linked under the table.

The short answer

There's no single best free screen recorder; it depends on which catch you can live with. Dotfik Studio is free in Chrome or Edge with no watermark, no fixed time limit and no uploads, but it has no share links. Loom and Castify are better for sharing and classrooms, but their free plans limit videos and length. Screen Studio is a paid Mac app.

Which free screen recorder should you choose?

Choose Dotfik Studio if you want a finished video without a catch

It suits tutorials, lessons and demos where you want your face in the corner, a few edits and an MP4 you can post anywhere, on any computer with Chrome or Edge. Nothing is uploaded and there's no account. Look elsewhere if you need share links, comments or a team library, or automatic zoom in desktop apps: its automatic zoom only follows clicks inside Chrome web pages, with the free extension, whether you share a tab, a window or the whole screen. Clicks in desktop apps aren't seen, so there you add zooms by hand.

Choose Loom if you mostly send quick messages by link

Loom is built around sharing. Comments, emoji reactions, viewer insights and team libraries are all in its free Starter plan. The catch is 25 videos of up to 5 minutes, at up to 720p, and no downloads unless you pay for Business (Loom pricing, checked 22 October 2026). Read Loom compared in detail.

Choose Castify if you teach with Google Classroom

Castify, the new name for Screencastify, connects to Google Classroom, Canvas and Schoology, and even the free plan can add questions and polls to your videos. Free videos carry a Castify watermark, and you get 10 of them, up to 30 minutes each (Castify pricing, checked 22 October 2026). Read Castify compared in detail.

Choose Screen Studio if you're on a Mac and polish matters most

Screen Studio is a Mac app that records your whole display, one window or an area, zooms in where you clicked, smooths the cursor and lets you edit by deleting sentences from the transcript. It costs $29 a month, or $9 a month on the yearly plan, and there's no Windows version (screen.studio, checked 22 October 2026). Read Screen Studio compared in detail.

Choose Cap if you want a free desktop app with automatic zoom

Cap is an open-source app for Mac, Windows and Linux that can "generate zooms from your recorded clicks", and its Studio Mode records straight to your device with no upload. The free version is for personal use only; commercial use needs a paid licence (Cap pricing, checked 22 October 2026).

Choose Screenity if you want an open-source Chrome extension

Screenity's free extension records your screen, camera and microphone, and the recording stays on your device with no account needed. Its editor, with automatic zoom, captions and MP4 export, is the paid part: $10 a month, or $120 billed yearly (screenity.io, checked 22 October 2026).

Choose Clipchamp if you want Microsoft's full video editor

Clipchamp is a video editor that also records your screen and webcam, in Edge or Chrome or as a Windows app. Free exports up to 1080p have no watermark, but each screen or webcam recording stops at 30 minutes, and you sign in with a Microsoft account (Microsoft support, checked 22 October 2026).

Choose OBS Studio for live streaming and complex scenes

OBS is free, open-source software "for video recording and live streaming" on Windows, Mac and Linux, with scenes you can switch between and an audio mixer with filters (obsproject.com, checked 22 October 2026). It takes time to set up, and its site doesn't describe a video editor, so plan to edit somewhere else.

Use your computer's built-in recorder for quick clips

A Chromebook records the screen with your camera and can show clicks and keys. A Mac records the screen with your microphone. The Windows 11 Snipping Tool records an area and can hand it to Clipchamp for editing. Google's and Apple's pages describe no editor for their recorders, and the files come out as WebM and MOV, which you can convert to MP4 and edit. What screen recorder do YouTubers use?

It depends on the video. For live streams, OBS Studio is made for the job, with scenes, sources and an audio mixer. For polished software demos on a Mac, Screen Studio adds zooms from your clicks and smooths the cursor for you. For tutorials and lessons with your face in the corner, a recorder that captures your screen, camera and voice in one take and then edits them, such as Dotfik Studio, saves a trip to a separate editor. Loom and Castify ask you to sign up, and Clipchamp asks you to sign in with a Microsoft account. The recorders built into Chromebooks, Macs and Windows 11 need no sign-up either.

Questions people ask

Which free screen recorder has no watermark and no time limit?

Dotfik Studio adds no watermark and sets no time limit, although very long recordings need plenty of free memory. Clipchamp's free exports up to 1080p have no watermark, but its recordings stop at 30 minutes; Castify's free plan adds a watermark, and Loom's stops each video at 5 minutes.

Do free screen recorders upload my videos?

Some do. Loom and Castify share your video by link from their own services, and Cap's Instant Mode uploads as you go. Dotfik Studio, OBS Studio and the built-in recorders save to your computer, and Clipchamp's personal accounts keep files on your computer unless you turn on content backup.

Which free screen recorder works on a school Chromebook?

The built-in recorder (Shift + Ctrl + Show windows) and browser-based recorders such as Dotfik Studio, Castify and Clipchamp all run on Chromebooks. A school can switch off screen capture on the Chromebooks it manages (Chrome Enterprise policy), so if recording fails, ask your IT team.

Is OBS Studio better than an online screen recorder?

For live streaming and complex scenes, often yes: it's free, open source and built for recording and live streaming. For a quick tutorial with your face and a few edits, a browser recorder is faster to start, because OBS needs installing and setting up and its site doesn't describe an editor.

Which free screen recorder is best for teachers?

If you rely on Google Classroom, questions inside videos and share links, Castify is built for schools, though its free plan has a watermark and a 10-video cap. If you want unlimited videos with no watermark and nothing uploaded, Dotfik Studio works on Chromebooks in Chrome, and you share the MP4 through Google Drive or Classroom yourself.
