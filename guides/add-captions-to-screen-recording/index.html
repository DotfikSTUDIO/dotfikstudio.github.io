How to add captions to a screen recording

Updated 22 October 2026 · 6-minute read

Captions help people who can't hear the sound, people watching with it off, and anyone who'd rather read along. This guide shows how to add them to a screen recording for free, burned into the video or as an .srt subtitle file for YouTube, without signing up anywhere.

The short answer

Make captions from your voice or type them, then either burn them into the video so they always show, or save them as an .srt subtitle file to upload to YouTube. Dotfik Studio does both free in Chrome, with no sign-up: the captions are made on your computer by the browser's own speech recognition, and your voice is never sent to an online service.

What's the difference between captions and subtitles?

In everyday use, both words mean text on screen that matches what's said. Strictly, captions are for people who can't hear the sound, so they're in the same language and can include sounds such as [music], while subtitles usually translate the speech for people who don't speak the language. For a screen recording, the bigger choice is how the text is delivered.

Should captions be burned in or a separate file?

Burned-in captions, also called open captions, are drawn into the picture. A caption file such as an .srt is a separate text file with a time for each line: the video player shows it over the video, and viewers can usually turn it off.

How do you add captions with Dotfik Studio?

  1. Record with your microphone on in the studio.
  2. In the editor, open the Captions settings and choose the language you spoke.
  3. Press Make captions from my voice. Your browser's on-device speech recognition listens to the recording in real time, so it takes as long as the recording. The first time, the browser may need to download a speech pack for that language.
  4. Read the captions through and fix any word it misheard: you can edit the text of any caption. You can also type captions by hand.
  5. To burn them in, turn on Show captions in the video. For YouTube, press Save .srt to download the subtitle file. Do this before you download the video, because by default the recording is erased from the page once the MP4 is saved.
  6. Download the MP4.

Captions can be made from your voice in English (US, UK and India), Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil), Hindi, Japanese and Korean. For any other language, type them.

How do you upload an .srt file to YouTube?

YouTube accepts SubRip (.srt) files (supported subtitle files). These are its steps for adding one on a computer (YouTube Help, checked 22 October 2026):

  1. Sign in to YouTube Studio and select Subtitles in the left menu.
  2. Select the video.
  3. Click ADD LANGUAGE and choose the language of your captions.
  4. Under Subtitles, click ADD, then select Upload file.
  5. Choose With timing, because an .srt file has the times in it, then Continue.
  6. Choose your .srt file and select Save.

Can YouTube make the captions for you?

Often, yes. YouTube "can use speech recognition technology to automatically create captions" in many languages. Its help page also says they "may not be ready at the time that you upload a video", asks creators to review them and fix mistakes in YouTube Studio, and lists reasons they may not appear: a video that's too long, poor sound, a long silence at the start, an unsupported language, or people talking over each other (YouTube Help, checked 22 October 2026).

Those captions stay on YouTube. If you post the same video elsewhere or send the file, it needs captions of its own.

Is your voice kept private?

With Dotfik Studio, yes. The captions are made by your browser's on-device speech recognition, and your voice is never sent to an online service. Recording, editing and the MP4 export all happen on your computer. The privacy page explains how, and how to check whether an online screen recorder is safe shows how to test any tool yourself.

What are the limits?

  • Browser. Making captions from your voice needs on-device speech recognition, which recent versions of Chrome have. If your browser can't do it, type the captions instead.
  • Languages. Only the languages listed above.
  • Time. It listens in real time, so a ten-minute recording takes about ten minutes.
  • Video files you open. For a file opened with Edit a video file instead, such as a Chromebook WebM, type the captions: making them from your voice works on the microphone sound of a recording made in the studio.
  • Accuracy. Speech recognition can mishear names and technical words, so read the captions through before you publish.

Questions people ask

Do YouTube Shorts need captions?

They aren't required. YouTube's help page covers automatic captions on long-form videos and Shorts and says it encourages creators to "add professional captions first" (YouTube Help, checked 22 October 2026). Burned-in captions are the safest choice for a Short, because they show wherever it's played.

How can I add subtitles to a video for free without signing up?

Open the studio in Chrome, choose Edit a video file instead, type the captions, turn on Show captions in the video to burn them in, and download the MP4. There's no account, and nothing is uploaded.

How do I make an .srt file from a video?

Make or type the captions in Dotfik Studio, then press Save .srt. For a recording made in the studio, Make captions from my voice writes them for you; for a video file you open, type them by hand.

Can I fix the captions before they're burned in?

Yes. You can edit the text of any caption in the editor, and the captions are burned into the MP4 when you download it.

Can I add captions on a Chromebook?

Yes, the studio runs in Chrome on a Chromebook. Making captions from your voice needs a recent version of Chrome; if it isn't available, type the captions instead. The Chromebook guide covers recording with sound.
