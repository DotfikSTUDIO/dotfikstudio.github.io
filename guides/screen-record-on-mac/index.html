How to screen record on a Mac with internal audio

Updated 22 October 2026 · 8-minute read

Every Mac can record its screen with one keyboard shortcut. Sound is the catch: Apple's tools record your microphone, not the Mac's own audio. This guide covers the built-in way, why that sound goes missing, and how to record it without installing an audio driver.

The short answer

Press Shift + Command + 5, choose the entire screen, a window or a portion, pick your microphone under Options and click Record. Stop with the Stop Recording button in the menu bar. Apple's tool records your voice, not the Mac's own sound. For that, use Dotfik Studio in Chrome 141 or newer on macOS 14.2 or newer, with no audio driver to install.

How do you screen record on a Mac?

The Mac's built-in Screenshot tool does it. These steps follow Apple's guide (checked 22 October 2026):

  1. Press Shift + Command + 5.
  2. In the toolbar, choose Record the entire screen, Record a window or Record a portion of the screen. For a portion, drag the frame or its edges.
  3. Click Options and pick your Microphone so it has a tick; None turns sound off. Here you can also choose where to save, set a timer and show the mouse pointer or clicks.
  4. Click Record. For a window, move the pointer over it and click.
  5. To stop, click the Stop Recording button in the menu bar.

The video is saved as a .mov file whose name starts with "Screen Recording", followed by the date and time. With Show Floating Thumbnail on, click the thumbnail in the corner to trim it.

Can you use QuickTime Player instead?

Yes, but it leads to the same place. In QuickTime Player, choose File > New Screen Recording and the same Screenshot tools open, with the same Options menu and Record button (Apple's QuickTime Player guide, checked 22 October 2026). So it has the same sound limits.

Can a Mac screen record internal audio?

Not with Apple's own tools, going by Apple's documentation. The only sound choice in Screenshot's Options is a microphone, and Apple's guide says nothing about recording the sound the Mac itself plays. A video, a game or an alert sound won't be in the recording, apart from whatever your microphone picks up from the speakers.

There are two ways round it: a virtual audio driver such as BlackHole, the long-standing workaround, or recording in Chrome, which can now capture the Mac's sound directly.

How do you record a Mac's internal audio without BlackHole?

From version 141, Chrome on macOS 14.2 or newer offers a "share system audio" switch when you share a window or the entire screen. Before that, only Chrome on Windows and ChromeOS could record system sound along with the screen (tested by addpipe, published 19 February 2026, checked 22 October 2026). The macOS version matters because, as the same article notes, Apple only let third-party apps capture system audio from macOS 14.2. Dotfik Studio runs in Chrome, so it can use that switch:

  1. Check your versions. In Chrome, open the three-dot More menu > Help > About Google Chrome, and select Relaunch if an update is waiting (Chrome Help). On the Mac, choose Apple menu > About This Mac; macOS Sonoma is version 14 (Apple Support). Both checked 22 October 2026.
  2. Open the studio in Chrome, choose your microphone and, if you like, your camera, and turn on Record computer sound.
  3. Choose a window or your entire screen, and turn on the share system audio switch in Chrome's picker.
  4. Press Start recording. When you stop, edit the video and press Download MP4.

On older versions, share a Chrome tab instead and tick Also share tab audio. You get that tab's sound, which works on every system and is enough for a video or web app playing in the tab.

What about BlackHole?

BlackHole, from Existential Audio, is "a modern macOS virtual audio loopback driver": it passes sound from one app to another. It's open source under the GPL-3.0 licence (BlackHole on GitHub), and its download page asks for an optional donation (both checked 22 October 2026).

The usual setup: install the driver, then in Audio MIDI Setup create a Multi-Output Device so you still hear the sound while it goes to BlackHole (Apple's Audio MIDI Setup guide), make it your sound output, and pick BlackHole as the microphone in Screenshot's Options. It works with any app, not just a browser. The costs are setup and a few quirks: BlackHole's notes say "macOS does not support changing the volume of a Multi-Output device", and recording your voice too means combining your microphone and BlackHole into an aggregate device (Apple Support; both checked 22 October 2026).

How do you let Chrome record the screen on a Mac?

macOS asks before an app can record your screen. If you chose Don't Allow, go to Apple menu > System Settings, click Privacy & Security, then Screen & System Audio Recording, and turn on Google Chrome (Apple's guide). The microphone has its own list under Privacy & Security > Microphone (Apple's guide; both checked 22 October 2026). After changing either, quit Chrome and open it again.

Why is there no sound on my Mac screen recording?

  1. No microphone was chosen. In Screenshot's Options, pick your microphone so it has a tick.
  2. You expected the Mac's own sound. Apple's tools record the microphone only. Use Chrome as described above, or BlackHole.
  3. The audio switch in Chrome's picker was off. It has to be on when you share. Dotfik Studio notices and asks you to choose what to share again and tick it.
  4. Chrome or macOS is too old. System sound from a window or the screen needs Chrome 141 or newer on macOS 14.2 or newer. Update, or share a tab instead.
  5. Chrome isn't allowed to record. Check Screen & System Audio Recording and Microphone in Privacy & Security, then quit and reopen Chrome.

How do you edit a Mac screen recording or make it an MP4?

For a quick trim, Apple's tools are enough: click the floating thumbnail after recording, or open the file in QuickTime Player, choose Edit > Trim and drag the yellow handles (Apple's trimming guide). QuickTime saves .mov files, though: "QuickTime Player doesn't export movies as MP4 videos" (Apple's export guide; both checked 22 October 2026).

For an MP4, or more editing, open the .mov in Dotfik Studio: choose Edit a video file instead on the start screen, cut out mistakes, add zooms by hand, blur private details or type captions, then press Download MP4. The conversion guide walks through it.

Questions people ask

Does QuickTime record internal audio?

Not according to Apple's guides. File > New Screen Recording opens the Screenshot tools, whose only sound option is a microphone.

Where do screen recordings go on a Mac?

Wherever Save to points in Screenshot's Options. Each one is a .mov file named Screen Recording with the date and time.

How do I stop a screen recording on a Mac?

Click the Stop Recording button in the menu bar at the top of the screen.

Can I record my screen and face at the same time on a Mac?

Apple's Screenshot tool has no camera option. Dotfik Studio records your screen, camera and microphone together in Chrome: see how to record your screen and face.

How do I record only part of the screen on a Mac?

Press Shift + Command + 5, choose Record a portion of the screen, then drag the frame or its edges around the area you want before you click Record.

Record your Mac's screen with your voice and its own sound, in Chrome, with no driver to install.
