How to record your screen and your face at the same time

Updated 22 October 2026 · 7-minute read

A tutorial, a lesson or a product demo feels more personal when people can see you talking. This guide shows the free way to record your screen with your face in the corner, on Windows, Mac, Chromebook or Linux, without installing anything.

The short answer

Use a screen recorder that records your screen and your webcam together. Dotfik Studio does it free in Chrome or Edge: open the studio, pick your camera and microphone, choose the screen to share and press Start recording. Afterwards you can move or resize your face, or switch to a split screen, then download an MP4. There is no sign-up and no watermark.

What you need

  • A computer with Chrome, Edge, Brave or another Chromium browser. Safari, Firefox and phones aren't supported yet.
  • A webcam: the one built into your laptop is fine.
  • A microphone. A headset or a USB microphone sounds clearer than a laptop's built-in one, but any will do.

How to record your screen and face with Dotfik Studio

  1. Open Dotfik Studio in your browser. Nothing is installed and you don't need an account.
  2. Under Camera and microphone, choose your camera and your microphone. Your browser asks for permission the first time; choose Allow. You'll see yourself in the preview.
  3. Choose what to share: your whole screen, one window, or a single browser tab. Your browser shows its own sharing window for this, so you always see exactly what is recorded. If you want the sound from your computer as well, turn on Record computer sound and tick the audio switch in that window.
  4. Pick a countdown (3, 5 or 10 seconds, or none) and press Start recording. A small floating bubble shows your camera while you talk, so you can keep an eye on your framing.
  5. Pause whenever you need to, then stop. The editor opens straight away.
  6. In the editor, choose how your face appears (see the layouts below), pick a video shape, trim the start and end, and press download. You get an MP4 file, made on your computer.

Your camera is recorded on its own, separately from the screen. That's why you can change its position, size and shape after recording instead of getting it right beforehand.

Can you do it with the tools already on your computer?

Sometimes. Here is what each system includes, according to its maker:

  • Chromebook: the built-in screen recorder has a Front Camera switch that shows your camera while you record (Google's help page, checked 22 October 2026). Videos are saved as WebM files, and there's no editor. Our Chromebook guide covers both ways.
  • Windows 11: Clipchamp, Microsoft's video editor, can record your screen and camera together. Its webcam and screen recordings are "currently limited to 30 minutes" (Microsoft support, checked 22 October 2026). The Snipping Tool records the screen and sound only. See how to screen record on Windows.
  • Mac: the Screenshot tool (Shift Command 5) records the screen and your microphone. Its options include no camera setting (Apple's guide, checked 22 October 2026). See how to screen record on a Mac.

A browser-based recorder works the same way on all of them, which helps if you switch between a school Chromebook and a home laptop.

How do YouTubers record their screen and themselves?

There are two common approaches. One is an app that records the screen and camera together and then edits them. The other is OBS Studio, a free, open-source app for Windows, Mac and Linux (obsproject.com), where you build a scene with your screen and a camera box. OBS is powerful but takes some setting up, and it's built for recording and live streaming rather than editing, so the editing usually happens in a second program.

Dotfik Studio sits in between: it records the screen, camera and voice in one take, then gives you the editing that tutorials usually need, such as zooming in on the important parts, cutting mistakes, removing long pauses and adding captions.

Tips for looking and sounding good

  • Light from the front. Sit facing a window or a lamp, not with one behind you.
  • Camera at eye level. Raise your laptop on a few books so the camera isn't looking up at you.
  • Quiet notifications. Turn on Do Not Disturb and close tabs with personal information before you share your screen.
  • Keep the noise down. Turn on Reduce background noise in the studio. In the editor, Even out my voice keeps the volume steady.
  • Record in short takes. A few short videos are easier to redo than one long one. If you do record in one go, Cut the pauses removes the long silences in one click.
  • Hide what shouldn't be seen. If an email address or a password appears, blur or cover that area in the editor for exactly as long as it's on screen.

Is it safe to record your face with an online recorder?

It depends on where the video goes. Many online recorders upload your video to their servers. Dotfik Studio doesn't: the recording, the editing and the MP4 export all happen in your browser, on your computer, and the recording is erased from the page when you're done. The privacy page explains how, and how to check whether an online screen recorder is safe shows how to test any recorder yourself.

Questions people ask

How do I record my screen and webcam at the same time on Windows 11?

Open Dotfik Studio in Chrome or Edge, choose your camera and microphone, pick the screen to share and press Start recording. Clipchamp, which comes with Windows 11, can also do it, with a 30-minute limit per recording.

Can I move my face to another corner after recording?

Yes. Dotfik Studio records the camera separately, so in the editor you can drag the bubble anywhere, resize it, change its shape or switch to a split screen.

Is there a free screen and face recorder without a watermark?

Dotfik Studio is free, adds no watermark and needs no account. It runs in Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers on Windows, Mac, Chromebook and Linux computers.

Can I record a tutorial without showing my face?

Yes. Choose No camera before you record, or pick the Screen layout in the editor afterwards. Your voice and the screen stay in the video.

Do I need to install an app or an extension?

No. Recording your screen, camera and voice works in the browser on its own. The optional Chrome extension adds automatic zooms and click effects in the browser tabs you record.
