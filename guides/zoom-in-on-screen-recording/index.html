How to zoom in on a screen recording (automatically or by hand)

Updated 22 October 2026 · 8-minute read

Zooms turn a wide, busy screen into something people can read, even on a phone. This guide shows how to zoom in on a screen recording automatically where you click, how to add zooms by hand to any recording, and how to show your clicks and make the cursor easy to follow.

The short answer

Record your screen, then add the zooms in an editor. Dotfik Studio does both free in Chrome or Edge: with its free extension, it zooms in automatically where you click inside the browser tabs you record. For desktop apps, or a video you already have, press Add zoom here at any moment and adjust the zoom on the timeline. There's no sign-up and no watermark.

Why zoom in on a screen recording?

A computer screen is wide and full of small text. In a small video player, or on a phone, a menu item or a line of code can shrink until nobody can read it. A zoom does two jobs: it makes the important part big enough to read, and it shows viewers where to look while you talk. A good zoom moves in just before the click that matters and back out once it's done, so nobody loses their place.

How do you zoom in automatically where you click?

To zoom where you click, a recorder has to know where you clicked. A web page that records your screen only receives the picture, so Dotfik Studio uses a free browser extension to tell it where your pointer is and when you click. The limit, plainly: automatic zooms work only for clicks inside the browser tabs you record, in Chrome, Edge, Brave or another Chromium browser. The extension lives in web pages, so it can't see clicks in apps installed on your computer, such as a photo editor or a code editor.

  1. Install the free extension. It takes about a minute, and the extension page has the steps.
  2. Open the studio, choose your camera and microphone if you want them, and share the tab you're going to work in.
  3. Record as usual. There's nothing special to do: just click normally.
  4. Stop. In the editor's Zoom settings, make sure Zoom in automatically where I click is on. Your clicks become zooms on the timeline.
  5. Play it back, fine-tune it with the settings below, and download the MP4.

Three settings change how the automatic zooms feel:

  • Follow my pointer while zoomed in. The zoomed view glides along with your pointer. Turn it off and the view holds still on the spot you clicked, which is calmer while you fill in a form.
  • Zoom out when my pointer moves away. The view zooms back out when you move your pointer away, so viewers see where you're heading next.
  • Default zoom. The zoom level: how far the zooms go in. A gentle zoom keeps some of the page around the click; a strong one makes small text readable on a phone.

How do you add a zoom by hand?

Adding zooms yourself works on any recording, with or without the extension. That includes recordings of desktop apps or your whole screen, and video files you open with Edit a video file instead on the studio's start screen, such as a Chromebook WebM or a Mac .mov recording.

  1. In the editor, move the playhead to the moment you want to zoom in.
  2. Press Add zoom here. A zoom appears on the timeline.
  3. To zoom in on a particular spot, select the zoom, press Aim at a spot I choose and click that spot in the preview.
  4. Drag the zoom along the timeline to move it, or drag its edges to change when it starts and ends. Select it to change its Zoom level or to delete it.

Automatic zooms can be changed the same way, so you can delete the one that zoomed in on an accidental click and keep the rest. To zoom in and out several times, add a zoom for each moment: between zooms, the view goes back out to the whole recording.

How do you show clicks and highlight the cursor?

With the free extension, Dotfik Studio can also draw these in the browser tabs you record. You'll find them in the editor's Pointer and keys settings, so you can switch each one on or off after recording:

  • Click ripples mark each click, so viewers notice it.
  • Pointer highlight keeps your pointer easy to find on a busy screen.
  • Spotlight dims everything except the area around your pointer.
  • Shortcut badges show the keyboard shortcuts you press, such as ⌘ K or Ctrl S. Normal typing is ignored unless you ask for it, and password fields are never read.

Like automatic zooms, these need the extension and work only in browser tabs.

Can you show clicks with the tools already on your computer?

Yes, and these also work outside the browser, because they're part of the system or a system tool. Here's what each maker documents (checked 22 October 2026):

ComputerShow clicksMake the pointer easier to see
Chromebook In the built-in screen recorder's settings, turn on Show clicks and keys. Only key combinations with modifier keys are shown (Google). Settings, then Accessibility: cursor highlighting, or a different cursor size or colour (Google).
Mac Press Shift Command 5. The Options menu lets you show the mouse pointer or clicks (Apple). System Settings, Accessibility, Display, Pointer: change the pointer's size and its outline and fill colours (Apple).
Windows Microsoft's free PowerToys includes Mouse Highlighter, which displays "visual indicators when you click". Win Shift H turns it on and off (Microsoft). Settings, Accessibility, Mouse pointer & touch: change the pointer's colour and size (Microsoft).

Make a ten-second test recording first to check they look the way you expect. The guides for Chromebook, Mac and Windows cover each system's own recorder.

Can you get automatic zooms in desktop apps?

Not from a web page, but some desktop apps do it. Screen Studio is a Mac app that records your whole display, one window or an area, then "zooms in on the moments that matter" based on where you clicked. It's "made for Mac and nothing else" and costs $29 a month, or $9 a month on the yearly plan (screen.studio, checked 22 October 2026). Our Screen Studio comparison goes through the differences.

On Windows, Microsoft's free ZoomIt, part of PowerToys, zooms in on part of the screen with a shortcut while you present: Ctrl 1 toggles zoom and Ctrl 4 toggles live zoom. It also has its own recorder (Microsoft, checked 22 October 2026). In Dotfik Studio, recordings of desktop apps get their zooms by hand.

Tips for zooms that help instead of distract

  • Match the zoom level to the content. Zoom further for small text and code, and less for a page layout where people need to see where things are.
  • Hold still while you type. If the view drifts while you fill in a form, turn off Follow my pointer while zoomed in.
  • Don't zoom on every click. Constant zooming is tiring to watch. Delete the zooms on clicks that don't matter, such as closing a pop-up.
  • Move the pointer slowly. A steady pointer gives the zoom time to follow and gives viewers time to read.
  • Start with bigger text. Sharing a browser tab or one window instead of a large monitor means less zooming later.
  • Check it on a phone. Watch the MP4 on a phone before you publish. If you squint, zoom further. For vertical videos, see how to turn a screen recording into a Short.

Questions people ask

How do I zoom in and out on a screen recording?

Add a zoom for each moment you want to zoom in; the view zooms back out when that zoom ends. In Dotfik Studio, press Add zoom here, then drag the zoom's edges on the timeline to set when it starts and ends.

Can I zoom in while I'm recording?

You don't need to. With the free extension, Dotfik Studio notes where you click in the browser tabs you record and turns those clicks into zooms afterwards, which you can still change. You can also add zooms by hand after recording.

Is there a free screen recorder with auto zoom?

Dotfik Studio is free, with no watermark and no sign-up, and its free Chrome extension adds automatic zooms for clicks inside the browser tabs you record. For desktop apps, add the zooms by hand.

Does automatic zoom work in desktop apps?

Not in Dotfik Studio: the extension only sees clicks inside web pages. Record the app, then add zooms with Add zoom here, which works on any recording.

Can I zoom in on a video I've already recorded?

Yes. On the studio's start screen, choose Edit a video file instead, open the video, add zooms by hand and download it as an MP4. Chromebook WebM files work too; see how to convert WebM to MP4.
