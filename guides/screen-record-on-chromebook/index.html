How to screen record on a Chromebook (with sound and your face)

Updated 22 October 2026 · 8-minute read

Every Chromebook has a screen recorder built in. This guide shows how to use it with sound and your camera, how to fix a recording that comes out silent, and the free way to edit it and save it as an MP4.

The short answer

Press Shift + Ctrl + Show windows and choose Screen record. Under Settings, pick Device audio, Microphone or both, and turn on Front Camera to include your face. Stop with Search + Shift + X. The video is saved as a WebM file in Downloads; to edit it or make an MP4, open it in Dotfik Studio, a free browser editor.

How do you screen record on a Chromebook?

The recorder is part of the screen capture tool. These steps follow Google's Chromebook help page (checked 22 October 2026):

  1. Press Shift + Ctrl + Show windows. You can also select the time at the bottom right, then Screen capture.
  2. In the bar at the bottom of the screen, select Screen record.
  3. Choose what to record: the full screen, a partial screen or one window.
  4. Select Settings to choose your sound and camera (see the next section).
  5. Start recording. For the full screen, click anywhere. For a partial screen, drag to select the area, then choose Record video. For a window, select the window.
  6. To stop, select Stop recording at the bottom right, or press Search + Shift + X.

The recording is saved as a .webm file in your Downloads folder. To save somewhere else, open the tool, select Settings and choose a folder.

How do you record with audio and your face (facecam)?

Before you start, select Settings in the capture bar. Google's page lists these choices:

  • Sound: Device audio (sound your Chromebook plays, such as a video), Microphone (your voice), or Device audio and microphone for both.
  • Your face: turn on Front Camera to record with your camera on.
  • Clicks and keys: turn on Show clicks and keys to show mouse clicks, touch gestures and keyboard shortcuts. To protect your privacy, it only shows key combinations that include a modifier key such as Ctrl, so ordinary typing isn't displayed.

One catch: a partial-screen recording can also be saved as a GIF, and "GIFs don't capture audio or video input", so choose Record video when you want sound.

Why does my Chromebook screen recording have no sound?

Work through these checks in order. They come from Google's help pages (all checked 22 October 2026).

  1. Pick a sound setting. In the capture tool's Settings, choose Device audio for sound from the Chromebook, Microphone for your voice, or both.
  2. Record a video, not a GIF. GIFs never have sound.
  3. Unmute your microphone. Select the time at the bottom right, then Audio settings next to the volume slider. Under Input, raise the microphone slider, and select the microphone icon if it's muted. Settings > Device > Audio lets you choose the right microphone and test it (volume help).
  4. Allow microphone access. In Settings > Privacy and security > Privacy controls, turn on Microphone access: when it's off, the microphone is blocked everywhere. If your Chromebook has a physical microphone switch, turn it on (privacy controls help).
  5. Update your Chromebook. If your settings don't match the ones above, go to Settings > About ChromeOS > Check for updates (update help).
  6. Recording in the browser? When Chrome asks what to share, the audio switch must be on: Also share tab audio for a tab, Also share system audio for the screen. Also check that Chrome hasn't blocked the microphone for the site, under Settings > Privacy and security > Site settings > Microphone (Chrome help).

Can you screen record on a school or work Chromebook?

Usually, but not always. On a Chromebook that a school or employer manages, an administrator can turn recording off:

  • In the Google Admin console, an administrator can choose Do not allow users to take screenshots or video recordings, which stops the built-in recorder (Google's guide for administrators, checked 22 October 2026).
  • Chrome has a separate policy, Allow or deny screen capture. When it's set to deny, websites' requests to share the screen fail unless the administrator allows that site, so browser recorders, Dotfik Studio included, can't record either (Chrome Enterprise policy list, checked 22 October 2026).

Both are deliberate choices by whoever manages the device. If recording is blocked, ask your teacher or IT team rather than looking for a way round it.

What does Dotfik Studio add on a Chromebook?

The built-in recorder is quick, but it gives you a WebM file and no editor. Dotfik Studio is a free recorder and editor that runs in Chrome, so it works on a Chromebook with nothing to install and no account. Nothing is uploaded: recording, editing and the MP4 export all happen on your Chromebook.

  1. Open the studio in Chrome.
  2. Choose your camera and microphone (each is optional) and allow access when Chrome asks.
  3. Choose what to share: a Chrome tab, a window or the whole screen. For the Chromebook's own sound, turn on Record computer sound and tick the audio switch in Chrome's sharing window: Also share tab audio for a tab, Also share system audio for a window or the screen.
  4. Press Start recording. A small floating bubble shows your camera while you talk. Pause or stop when you're done.
  5. Edit the video, then press Download MP4.

What you get on top of the built-in tool:

  • Editing: trim the ends, cut out mistakes, speed up slow parts, and blur an email address or password for exactly as long as it's on screen.
  • Your face, your way: the camera is recorded separately, so afterwards you can choose a bubble, a split screen, only the camera or only the screen. See how to record your screen and face.
  • Zoom: add a zoom by hand at any moment. With the free Chrome extension, zooms, click ripples and key badges happen by themselves, but only for clicks inside the Chrome tabs you record. More in how to zoom in on a screen recording.
  • Captions: made from your voice by recent Chrome's on-device speech recognition, or typed by hand (how to add captions).
  • MP4: H.264 video with AAC sound, in 16:9 for YouTube or 9:16 for Shorts, up to 4K.

The trade-off: until you download it, the recording is kept in memory, up to about 40 MB per minute at 1080p. If your Chromebook has little free memory, record in shorter takes.

How do you edit a screen recording on a Chromebook?

Google's help page for the built-in recorder describes no editing tools. To edit a recording you've already made:

  1. Open Dotfik Studio in Chrome.
  2. On the start screen, choose Edit a video file instead and pick the .webm file from Downloads.
  3. Trim it, cut out parts, add zooms by hand, blur private details or type captions.
  4. Press Download MP4 and save it under a new name.

The WebM to MP4 guide covers this in detail, including how to keep the video plain when you only want to convert it.

Google also offers a Screencast app on Chromebooks. It records your screen with a transcript, stores recordings in Google Drive and has tools to trim, mute and add titles. Recording needs a supported device language, and administrators can turn it off (Google's Screencast guide, checked 22 October 2026).

Questions people ask

Does the Chromebook screen recorder record audio?

Yes, if you choose Device audio, Microphone or both under Settings before you start. GIF recordings never have sound.

Where do screen recordings go on a Chromebook?

They're saved as .webm files in your Downloads folder. You can pick another folder in the capture tool's Settings.

How do I stop a screen recording on a Chromebook?

Select Stop recording at the bottom right of the screen, or press Search + Shift + X.

How do I convert a Chromebook recording to MP4?

Open Dotfik Studio in Chrome, choose Edit a video file instead, pick the .webm file and press Download MP4. The WebM to MP4 guide has the details.

Can I record only the Chromebook's sound, without my voice?

Yes. In the built-in recorder, choose Device audio instead of Microphone. In Dotfik Studio, choose no microphone and turn on Record computer sound.
